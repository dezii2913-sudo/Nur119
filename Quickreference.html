⚖️ Weight Conversions
🔢Pounds → Kilograms
kg = lbs ÷ 2.2
Example: 44 lbs ÷ 2.2 = 20 kg
Most common for weight-based dosing
🍼Grams → Kilograms (Neonates/Infants)
kg = g ÷ 1000
Example: 2200 g ÷ 1000 = 2.2 kg
Example: 4600 g ÷ 1000 = 4.6 kg
Always first step for birth weight problems!
🔄kg → lbs & oz (Baby Weight Reports)
Step 1: kg × 2.2 = total lbs (decimal)
Step 2: Whole # = pounds
Step 3: Decimal × 16 = ounces
2.8 kg: 2.8 × 2.2 = 6.16 → 6 lbs, 0.16 × 16 = 2.56 ≈ 3 oz
4.6 kg: 4.6 × 2.2 = 10.12 → 10 lbs, 0.12 × 16 = 1.92 ≈ 2 oz
4.8 kg: 4.8 × 2.2 = 10.56 → 10 lbs, 0.56 × 16 = 8.96 ≈ 9 oz
Round ounces to nearest whole oz
➕lbs & oz → kg (For Dosing)
Step 1: oz ÷ 16 = decimal lbs
Step 2: Add whole lbs
Step 3: Total lbs ÷ 2.2 = kg
8 lbs 12 oz: 12÷16 = 0.75; 8+0.75 = 8.75; 8.75÷2.2 = 3.98 kg
11 lbs 4 oz: 4÷16 = 0.25; 11.25÷2.2 = 5.11 kg
✅ Safe & Therapeutic (S&T) Verification
📋Step-by-Step S&T Process
1Convert weight to kg (lbs ÷ 2.2 or g ÷ 1000)
2Calculate actual mg/day: dose × doses per day
3Look up recommended dose range from drug reference
4Low safe dose = low mg/kg/day × kg
5High safe dose = high mg/kg/day × kg
6Actual ≥ low and ≤ high → ✅ S&T
Outside range → ❌ QUESTION THE ORDER!
⚠️ ALWAYS verify S&T BEFORE giving any pediatric dose!
📐Key Formulas
Actual mg/day = dose × doses/day

Low safe = low mg/kg/day × kg
High safe = high mg/kg/day × kg

mg/kg/day received = total mg/day ÷ kg

Single dose = (mg/kg/day × kg) ÷ doses/day

Volume to give = (Ordered ÷ Available mg) × Volume

✏️Worked Example 1 — S&T ✅
Order: 0.2 g IVPB q4h | 31 kg child
Range: 15–60 mg/kg/24h

0.2 g = 200 mg; q4h = 6 doses
Actual: 200 × 6 = 1,200 mg/day
Low: 15 × 31 = 465 | High: 60 × 31 = 1,860
1,200 is between 465–1,860 → ✅ SAFE
❌Worked Example 2 — NOT S&T
Order: 575 mg IVPB TID | 25 kg child
Range: 20–50 mg/kg/24h

TID = 3 doses; 575 × 3 = 1,725 mg/day
Low: 20 × 25 = 500 | High: 50 × 25 = 1,250
1,725 > 1,250 → ❌ UNSAFE — too high!
HOLD the dose — call the provider!
💡Worked Example 3 — Single Dose + Volume
Order: 100–200 mg/kg/24h ÷ q6h | 2200 g infant

Weight: 2200 ÷ 1000 = 2.2 kg
Low/day: 100 × 2.2 = 220 mg | High: 200 × 2.2 = 440 mg
q6h = 4 doses/day
Low single: 220 ÷ 4 = 55 mg
High single: 440 ÷ 4 = 110 mg
💧 Pediatric Fluid Maintenance (Holliday-Segar)
⚠️Critical Rule
NOT for neonatal use! — Children only (beyond neonatal period)
mL/hr = Total 24h mL ÷ 24
Round to nearest whole number
📊Holliday-Segar Formula Table
Weight24-Hour Formula
0–10 kg100 mL × kg
>10–20 kg1000 mL + 50 mL × (kg − 10)
>20 kg1500 mL + 20 mL × (kg − 20)
Memory trick: 100 / 50 / 20 → each tier, cut the rate in half(ish)
✏️Worked Examples
4300 g → mL/hr:
4.3 kg × 100 = 430 mL ÷ 24 = 18 mL/hr

5300 g → 24h:
5.3 kg × 100 = 530 mL/24h

38 kg → 24h:
1500 + (20 × 18) = 1860 mL/24h

11 lbs → 24h:
11 ÷ 2.2 = 5 kg → 5 × 100 = 500 mL/24h

44 lbs → mL/hr:
44 ÷ 2.2 = 20 kg → 1000 + (50 × 10) = 1500 ÷ 24 = 63 mL/hr

16 kg → mL/hr:
1000 + (50 × 6) = 1300 ÷ 24 = 54 mL/hr

❌ Using >20 formula for exactly 20 kg child

❌ Multiplying ALL kg by extra rate
   (only multiply the EXTRA kg above threshold)

🔍 Step It Out — How to Solve mL/day & mL/dose Questions
These questions all follow the same pattern. Once you learn the steps, every version is the same problem with different numbers. Work through this interactive example and the formula will click.
📋 Exam Question (like yours)
"The recommended dosage is 4 mg/kg/day in 3 divided doses. The child weighs 18 kg. You have 6 mg/2 mL available. How many mL would the child receive each day?"
Step 1 Find the total mg needed per day
Total mg/day = mg/kg/day × weight (kg)
Plug in:   4 mg/kg/day × 18 kg = ?
mg/day
💡 Why this step? The dose is written per kilogram, so you multiply by the child's weight to get the total amount their body needs in a day.
Step 2 Find the concentration (mg per mL)
Concentration = mg on label ÷ mL on label
Plug in:   6 mg ÷ 2 mL = ?
mg/mL
💡 Why this step? The label gives you mg and mL together. You need to know how many mg are in each mL so you can figure out how much liquid to draw up.
Step 3 Calculate mL/day using D/H × Q
mL/day = Total mg/day (Desired) ÷ mg available (Have) × Q (volume)
Plug in:   72 mg ÷ 6 mg × 2 mL = ?
(Shortcut: 72 ÷ 3 mg/mL = same answer)
mL/day
💡 D/H × Q: Desired ÷ Have × Quantity. This is the universal formula for any “how many mL” question. Desired = what you need, Have = concentration on label, Q = the volume that comes with it.
Bonus Step If the question asks mL/dose instead of mL/day
mL/dose = mL/day ÷ number of doses per day
Plug in:   24 mL/day ÷ 3 doses = ?
mL/dose
💡 Key distinction: “Per day” stops at Step 3. “Per dose” adds this division. Read the question carefully — it will say each day or per dose / each dose.
